      Guide

      What Trends shows

      Every player prop a sportsbook is offering on today's and tomorrow's NBA and NFL games, with the player's real results at that number. Each card is one line: the player, the game, the stat and the number, the best price and the book offering it, the hit rate for the period you chose, and a bar for each game in that period. Green bars cleared the number, red bars missed it, grey bars landed on it exactly.

      A game stays on the tab while it is being played and leaves once it is over. Tomorrow's games appear as soon as the books post lines on them, usually the evening before.

      Where the numbers come from

      Lines and prices come from the sportsbooks through The Odds API. Results come from ESPN's game logs. A hit rate counts the games in the period where the player reached the number. Last 20 means the player's last 20 games, including the end of last season early in the year. Head to head means every logged game against the opponent he plays today. The season periods count only that season.

      Lines are refreshed three times a day for the NBA and twice a day for the NFL. The time at the top of the tab is when the lines were last pulled, so a price can have moved since.

      Period, Filters and Sort

      The three buttons at the bottom of the screen control the whole tab. Period picks which games the hit rate and the bars cover: head to head, last 5, last 10, last 20, or a season. Sort orders the cards by hit rate, by price, or by game time, in either direction. The tab opens sorted by hit rate with the 100% lines first.

      Filters narrow the list. Markets limits it to the stats you want. Games limits it to certain games. Odds keeps only prices inside a range. Period Hit Rate hides every line under the rate you set, so 90% and up shows only the lines that almost always land. Side keeps overs or unders only. The search box matches players, teams and markets.

      Your sportsbooks

      Open Filters, then Sportsbooks, and tick the books you have an account with. From then on a line shows only if one of your books is offering it, at the best price among them, and the price on the card is one you can actually bet. With no book ticked, every book counts. Your choice is saved to your account, so it applies on every device you sign in from.

      The detail sheet

      Tap a card to open it. The tabs along the top show the hit rate in every period at once. Below them is the split of hits and misses, the average and the median for that period, the chart with the value of every game and the opponent under each bar, the game log with dates and finals, and every sportsbook's price on the line.

      Pixel's Picks

      Pixel's Picks are the Trends lines that pass a fixed test, then a research check. The test does not change: the line must have landed in every one of the player's last 20 games, at least ten of them logged, and in every game against today's opponent if he has played them before. One line per player and stat qualifies, the best paying of his 100% lines.

      Each qualifying line is then researched before it can post. Is the player on limited minutes, measured as his last three games against his last twenty? Is he out, doubtful or questionable on the injury report? Is he a starter or a bench player? Does ESPN project a blowout that would sit him in the fourth quarter? Is a teammate out, which changes who gets the ball? Is there a defender or a scheme that hinders this line? If any answer goes against the line, it passes and the tab says why. If everything comes back favorable, it posts, and anything still worth knowing goes in the note under the pick.

      The lines that pass become the day's tickets, priced only at the books Pixel plays: FanDuel, DraftKings, Caesars, Hard Rock, PrizePicks, bet365, Fanatics, BetMGM, Sleeper and Underdog. The first choice is a two leg parlay on one book as close to +100 as the lines allow, three legs when that is what fits under +150. Otherwise a straight bet between -200 and +150. A two leg ticket at +100 beats one straight at -200. At most two tickets a day, one unit each, and a unit is five dollars. PrizePicks takes no single picks, so a PrizePicks ticket is a lineup of different players from at least two teams: a 2-pick Power Play paid 3x, or a 3-pick Flex Play paid 2.25x with all three in and 1.25x with two. Some days it is none, and the tab says what qualified and what the research found.

      Every ticket is graded the morning after as one bet, the way the book settles it: a lost leg loses the ticket, a pushed or voided leg drops out and the ticket pays at the legs left. The record at the bottom of the tab keeps the running total in units and dollars, a graph of it by day you can hover or tap to see each day's tickets and what won or lost, and the full history, filtered by NBA, NFL or both.

      American odds, in plain terms

      A minus number such as -150 means bet 150 to win 100: the book thinks the line is more likely than not to land. A plus number such as +120 means bet 100 to win 120: the book thinks it is less likely than not. A 100% hit rate at -300 is a line the market already expects to land; a 100% hit rate at -110 is the one worth a closer look.

      About

      I'm Perpetual Pixel. I work in IT by day and I bet on sports most of the hours I'm not working. I built this because the research I wanted to do before a bet took too long by hand: pull up a player's last twenty games, check how often he cleared a number, check what he did against tonight's opponent, then find which book has the best price. Perpetual Picks does that for every player prop on the board, every day, and shows the work.

      Trends is the whole site. Every line a sportsbook is offering on today's and tomorrow's NBA and NFL games, with the player's real hit rate at that number over the period you choose, sorted so the lines that always land come first. Pixel's Picks are the lines that meet one fixed test, a hundred percent over the last twenty and against today's opponent, and then survive a research check on minutes, injuries, role, blowout risk and matchup.       Ladder Challenge Tracked as climbs, not as a win rate — the bankroll compounds

      One player-prop parlay a day of deep lines, priced −200 to +100 combined, staking the whole ladder bankroll; a loss resets the climb to $20. How a climb works

      One parlay a day of two or three low-level player props from NFL, NBA and NHL games — a line at 60% of the player's season average or lower, cleared in 85% of games this season and 80% of the last five, each leg −500 to −150, never two legs on one player — priced −200 to +100 combined, every leg at a book you can bet. Only one rung rides at a time: the next day's rung posts once the last has settled. A player who doesn't play drops out and the ticket pays the rest. A win rides straight into the next rung; a loss ends that climb and the next one starts back at $20, rung 1. Profit is skimmed off at fixed points on the way up, and that banked money is all a busted climb keeps — which is why each run is shown start to finish rather than averaged into anything.

      Daily Learning Automatic, reviews every morning before the day's picks lock in

      Every morning, before picks lock, the app re-weights its own misfiring segments — bounded to x0.70–x1.05. How this works

      The self-correction loop. Every morning at the 2am ET batch, before any picks are chosen, the app reviews its trailing 30 days of graded results — both actual wins vs. what its own probabilities predicted, and closing-line value — for every sport + bet-type segment and odds band. A segment that's been misfiring gets a bounded score penalty (down to x0.70) so it needs a visibly better number to make the board; one that's been sharp gets a small nudge (capped at x1.05). Small samples barely move the needle by design, adjustments apply only to the next day's selection, and the Full Slate record is never adjusted — it keeps measuring the raw engine so the evidence this learns from stays unbiased.
