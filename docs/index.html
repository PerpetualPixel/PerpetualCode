Guide

American odds, in plain terms

A minus number (like -150) is the favorite: bet $150 to win $100. A plus number (like +150) is the underdog: bet $100 to win $150. The bigger the minus number, the more likely that side is thought to win, and the less it pays out if it does.

What "Confidence" means

Every price gets graded 0–100. It isn't a guess at who wins, it's how good the price is relative to what the betting market itself thinks is fair, once you strip out the sportsbook's own fee. A higher confidence means a bigger gap between what you're being offered and what the market's own consensus says the bet is actually worth.

"Beats X% of the board" shows where that grade ranks against every other bet available right now, useful when you're choosing between a few decent-looking picks.

No-vig fair value and expected value (EV)

Sportsbooks build a small fee into every price, the "vig." Strip it out across enough books and you get a consensus for what a bet is actually worth, fee-free. If the best price you can find beats that fair value, you have positive expected value: over many bets like this you'd expect to come out ahead, even though any single bet can still lose.

Why every pick shows several sportsbooks

The same bet is often priced slightly differently at different books. "Line shopping" (checking several and taking the best number) costs nothing extra and is one of the most reliable ways to improve results over time. Every pick shows a button per sportsbook; greyed-out ones simply aren't offering that exact line.

Suggested stake, and what "¼-Kelly" means

The Kelly Criterion is a formula for how much of your bankroll to risk based on how big the edge is. Betting the full amount it suggests produces wild bankroll swings, so this app uses a quarter of that number instead: smoother, and still scaled to the size of the edge. A bigger suggested stake means a bigger edge, not just a bigger favorite.

Set a bankroll and a unit size under the Bankroll button to see that suggestion as a recommended number of units to bet instead of just a percentage.

Bankroll and units

A "unit" is a fixed slice of your bankroll (usually 1-2%) used as your basic betting increment instead of raw dollars. Betting "1.5 units" instead of "$30" keeps stakes proportional as your bankroll grows or shrinks, and it's how most bettors track their own results.

Closing Line Value (CLV)

The single best long-run signal that you're actually finding good bets, separate from whether any one of them won: did you get a better price than the market eventually settled on? The Tracking Dashboard's server-tracked picks show an Avg CLV figure for exactly this.

The four tabs

Full Slate shows every game in a league with a price on it, nothing hidden by odds or confidence, with a glowing ring around whichever side the algorithm leans toward on each market. Tap any price to see the case for it, and the case against it. Pixel's Picks is fully automatic: 5 locks, sharp range −250/+250, ranked purely by edge, generated once daily at 2am ET and locked in for the day. Nothing to press, and nothing changes after it's posted. Play of the Day is one pick, chosen once a day and the same for everyone, with a full written breakdown instead of the compact card.

How picks are made, step by step

1. Every priced game becomes a candidate. For each league, every market (moneyline, spread, total) on every live sportsbook quote is pulled in, nothing is excluded up front by odds or "gut feel." Each book's own price is stripped of its built-in fee (de-vigged) to get that book's honest read, then every book except the one offering the best price is combined into a consensus fair value. Excluding the best price keeps it from inflating its own benchmark, which would make every bet look artificially profitable.

2. Each candidate is graded 0–100. The best available price is compared against that consensus to get its real expected value (EV), the dominant factor (45% of the grade). The rest is confidence that the edge is real rather than a fluke of a thin or stale market: how many books agree (18%), how tightly they agree (15%), how much better the best price is than the field average, "line shopping" gain (14%), and how fresh the quote is relative to kickoff (8%). For team sports only (never MMA, see below), a small, capped adjustment from recent form, head-to-head history, and injury news can nudge the grade up to about 8 points either way; it can sharpen a close call but can't manufacture an edge that isn't there in the price.

3. Long-shot and heavy-favorite prices are filtered out. Only prices between −250 and +150 are considered at all. Anything more extreme is excluded regardless of how good its grade looks, since the Kelly math and win-rate assumptions this app runs on stop being reliable that far from even money. A pick also has to clear a minimum real edge (at least 1.5% EV) and a minimum suggested stake (a rounding-error-sized edge doesn't count even if the score looks decent). Scoring well on liquidity and freshness alone isn't enough; the price itself has to actually be worth taking.

4. Only today's games are eligible. A team-sport game has to be happening today (same ET calendar date) to be considered. A real edge on an NFL line for a game months out never gets to compete with tonight's board. MMA gets its own version of this window (today, or before 6am ET the next morning, since a late main event can run past midnight) since fight cards don't follow the same daily schedule as league games.

5. The best-graded picks are selected. Full Slate ranks live by score. Pixel's Picks is different: once a day at 2am ET, the server locks in the 5 best picks that clear every filter above and posts them for the whole day. Reloading the page later never changes them, and nothing about the picks reacts to price movement afterward. On a quiet day where fewer than 5 real edges clear the bar, the rest are filled from the next-best available prices and explicitly flagged "Outside standard criteria", still real, gradeable bets, just not sharp enough to call a lock without saying so. Every one of the 5 counts toward the Tracking Dashboard's win-rate math regardless; the flag is transparency about how the pick was chosen, not an exclusion.

6. The write-up explains the pick that was already made. It never re-decides. Once a side is chosen by the math above, the AI matchup analysis is told which side that is and asked to build the strongest honest case for it from real facts (form, head-to-head, injuries), plus genuine risks specific to that pick. It is never asked to independently guess who's favored, so it can't contradict the pick shown on the card. MMA gets no qualitative scoring at all (see step 2); Sherdog/UFC.com research is shown for context, but an MMA pick, underdog or favorite, is chosen purely on the price math in steps 1-3, and the "Market & Price Case" section always stays visible for MMA specifically so that reasoning is never hidden behind the AI's fighter-facts write-up.

About

Pixel Pick prices every game on the board against the market's own no-vig consensus (the same math a sharp bettor would do by hand, run across every book at once) and surfaces the real edges: Full Slate for every market on every game, Pixel's Picks for the sharpest 5 locks, and Play of the Day for one highest-conviction pick, posted automatically each morning. Every tracked pick is graded against its actual result, win or lose, so the Tracking Dashboard reflects real performance, not a marketing number.
