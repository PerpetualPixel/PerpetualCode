Guide

American odds, in plain terms

A minus number (like -150) is the favorite: bet $150 to win $100. A plus number (like +150) is the underdog: bet $100 to win $150. The bigger the minus number, the more likely that side is thought to win — and the less it pays out if it does.

What "Confidence" means

Every price gets graded 0–100. It isn't a guess at who wins — it's how good the price is relative to what the betting market itself thinks is fair, once you strip out the sportsbook's own fee. A higher confidence means a bigger gap between what you're being offered and what the market's own consensus says the bet is actually worth.

"Beats X% of the board" shows where that grade ranks against every other bet available right now — useful when you're choosing between a few decent-looking picks.

No-vig fair value and expected value (EV)

Sportsbooks build a small fee into every price — the "vig." Strip it out across enough books and you get a consensus for what a bet is actually worth, fee-free. If the best price you can find beats that fair value, you have positive expected value: over many bets like this you'd expect to come out ahead, even though any single bet can still lose.

Why every pick shows several sportsbooks

The same bet is often priced slightly differently at different books. "Line shopping" — checking several and taking the best number — costs nothing extra and is one of the most reliable ways to improve results over time. Every pick shows a button per book you've selected under Sportsbooks; greyed-out ones simply aren't offering that exact line.

Suggested stake, and what "¼-Kelly" means

The Kelly Criterion is a formula for how much of your bankroll to risk based on how big the edge is. Betting the full amount it suggests produces wild bankroll swings, so this app uses a quarter of that number instead — smoother, and still scaled to the size of the edge. A bigger suggested stake means a bigger edge, not just a bigger favorite.

Set a bankroll under the Bankroll button to see that suggestion as a real dollar amount or a number of units instead of just a percentage.

Bankroll and units

A "unit" is a fixed slice of your bankroll — usually 1–2% — used as your basic betting increment instead of raw dollars. Betting "1.5 units" instead of "$30" keeps stakes proportional as your bankroll grows or shrinks, and it's how most bettors track their own results.

Closing Line Value (CLV)

The single best long-run signal that you're actually finding good bets, separate from whether any one of them won: did you get a better price than the market eventually settled on? History tracks this automatically for every pick that was still open when its game started, and shows your running average at the top of the History panel.

The four tabs

Full Slate shows every game in a league with a price on it — nothing hidden by odds or confidence — with a glowing ring around whichever side the algorithm leans toward on each market. Tap any price to see the case for it, and the case against it. Pixel Picks is automatic: tap Generate and get the 8 best-graded bets across every sport you've selected, restricted to a −200/+150 odds band by default, ranked purely by edge. Parlay Builder is manual: toggle on the sports and markets you want, set your own odds range and confidence floor, and build a ticket by hand. Play of the Day is one pick, chosen once a day and the same for everyone, with a full written breakdown instead of the compact card.

Odds range and confidence floor

By default the Board only shows bets priced between −250 and +150, graded 50 or higher. Both are adjustable under "Odds & Confidence" — a thin sport on a quiet night clearing nothing at those settings isn't a bug, it's the filter doing its job. Widen the range or lower the floor to see more of the board.
